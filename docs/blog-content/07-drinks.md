Choose a drink at Three Monkeys Phuket by starting with the flavour you want: fruity, tart, creamy, tea-based or coffee-led. The menu includes several non-alcoholic categories, so a drink does not need alcohol to feel like part of the occasion. Check the current drinks selection and ask about ingredients or sweetness before deciding, especially if the name is unfamiliar.

The most photogenic glass is not necessarily the one you will enjoy with your meal. Think about what you are eating, how thirsty you are and whether you want a drink to sip slowly or something simpler alongside food. A little attention to those questions can be more useful than choosing purely from the garnish.

## Read the drinks menu in categories

The [menu page](/menu) has a drinks selection alongside food. Categories help narrow the options: juices, fruit shakes, mocktails, teas and coffees offer different textures and flavours. Start with the category you are in the mood for, then look at individual descriptions. If a drink's name does not tell you much, ask the team to explain it.

Distinguish a juice from a blended shake, and a tea from a sweet mixed drink using tea as one ingredient. The finished texture, sweetness and serving style can be quite different. If you have a strong preference about ice, sweetness or milk, ask whether an adjustment is possible rather than assuming every drink can be changed in the same way.

## Mocktails can be part of the occasion

Mango Coco appears in the restaurant's mocktail selection, alongside other named choices. Its photograph shows a yellow drink with a mango garnish, but a photograph cannot establish the complete recipe. Ask which ingredients are included if you are avoiding a fruit, coconut, dairy or another component.

A fruit-led mocktail can suit a leisurely meal or a celebration where you want something visually distinctive. It may also be a richer or sweeter part of the order than you expect. If you are already choosing a rich main or dessert, consider whether you want that combination or would prefer a lighter drink.

For guests avoiding alcohol completely, say so plainly when ordering and ask about the ingredients of the chosen drink. Do not rely on a creative name or a decorative glass to establish what is inside. The current menu category and the team's explanation are the useful references.

## Fresh coconut and fruit drinks

Fresh coconut is listed in the juice selection. The venue photograph shows it served in the shell, which gives it a different presentation from a glass of juice or a blended drink. Check availability when ordering, and ask about any additions if you want to understand exactly what will be served.

Fruit drinks can play several roles in a meal. Some diners want a familiar flavour before choosing food; others want something refreshing to accompany an afternoon visit. Ask whether the drink is pressed, squeezed or blended, and whether it contains additional ingredients. Those details matter more than assuming that every fruit-based drink has the same preparation.

## Tea is worth considering with food

The Three Monkeys tea selection includes oolong and other named teas. If you prefer tea to a fruit drink, ask about the available serving style and whether it is sweetened. The photographed oolong is presented over ice, but the current menu and team should guide the options available during your visit.

Tea can be an appealing choice when you want to sip something alongside the conversation without ordering another substantial course. Different teas have different aromas and levels of intensity. Ask for a description if you are choosing between an unfamiliar tea and a flavour you already know you enjoy.

Do not assume that a tea, coffee or a drink described with botanical ingredients is caffeine-free. If caffeine matters to your choice, ask about the specific preparation. Likewise, discuss any dietary requirement directly rather than using a drink's colour or category as a shortcut.

## Choose alongside the food, not in isolation

There is no single correct pairing for a Thai meal. Your preferences matter, and a shared table may contain several different flavours at once. A useful starting point is to decide whether you want the drink to contrast with the food or repeat a flavour you already enjoy.

For example, if much of the meal is rich, you might prefer something less creamy. If the food has strong sour flavours, decide whether you enjoy more tartness in the drink or would rather choose a gentler flavour. These are preferences, not rules. Ask about the actual drink rather than making assumptions from the menu photograph.

## Keep celebration arrangements clear

If drinks are mentioned in a celebration package, read the exact inclusion. A specified bottle or a stated non-alcoholic substitution is not an unlimited drinks arrangement. Confirm the available choice, quantity and any substitution before booking if it affects the occasion.

For a group, avoid choosing everyone's drink without checking preferences. Some guests may want tea, water or a familiar juice rather than the same mixed drink as the rest of the table. Give people room to choose, and make any important dietary information clear to the team.

## Questions before ordering

### Are there non-alcoholic choices?

Yes. The menu has mocktails, fruit drinks, teas and coffees. Check the current selection and ask about the particular preparation you want.

### Can sweetness be adjusted?

Ask about the chosen drink. Some recipes or prepared ingredients may allow less flexibility than others, so an adjustment should be confirmed rather than assumed.

### Can I order the drink shown in a photograph?

Use the photograph as a starting point, then check the name and current availability on the menu. Presentation and ingredients should be confirmed when they are important to your choice.

Explore the [drinks menu](/menu) and choose something that suits your appetite and evening. If you are still planning the visit, [book your table](/booking) and leave space to decide the final details when you arrive.
